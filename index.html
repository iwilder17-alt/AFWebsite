How I work

Most sellers spray accounts. I earn one real conversation — and I engineer the system that finds it.

  1. 01

    The unit of work is a person.

    If the segment isn't already people, I source contacts before I send a word. Accounts don't reply — people do.

  2. 02

    Guardrails are the product.

    A SKIP rule that kills a send beats fake personalization every time. Declining an available automation is itself a guardrail.

  3. 03

    I don't claim loops I didn't close.

    Honest post-mortems over vanity metrics. If the send didn't fire, the write-up says so — and says why.

  4. 04

    Validate the pipe before you build the machine.

    Stress-test the delivery mechanism with one test send before investing in the upstream build. Learned this the hard way.

Case 01 AlphaForge · Harvey (legal AI)

A connected outbound motion into the legal-AI market — fired on judgment, not volume.

Nine named innovation executives. One honest, single-touch email each, sequenced from my own connected mailbox — recognizably me, not infrastructure. Segment, enrichment, per-lead personalization, guardrails and tracking, all built and inspectable. The interesting part isn't that it sends. It's everything I built to decide who not to send to.

The move

Of three segments, I shipped Top-Down Persona — the only one where the unit of work was already a person. Ten named Chief Innovation Officers, verified titles, 100 match scores. The honest framing — "GTM engineer studying Harvey's market, would love your perspective" — is a natural message to a CINO. Reading how the market perceives legal AI is literally their job. Signal-Hot and Displacement were account-level; I'd have to source contacts before sending a word.

What I built

  • Work-email waterfall → verification per lead
  • Claygent research: each recipient's most recent public statement on legal AI — with a hard SKIP rule (no specific finding = no send)
  • Pre-emptive blocklist screen & per-row manual approval gate
  • 10 messages, each anchored to the recipient's own words, <120 words, effortless opt-out
  • Native email sequencing from my connected mailbox — single-touch, no follow-up in the sequence at all
  • Auto-reply deliberately disabled — at this volume, every reply deserved a human

Tracking design, written before the build: a positive outcome is any substantive reply — a perspective, or a correction of my thesis. Not meetings; that incentive corrupts the message at this volume.

The ethics call

Several candidates are active advocates of the incumbent, Legora, with recent public statements praising it. Contacting them as a transparent GTM researcher — across a multinational TAM with GDPR/LGPD jurisdictions in play — is legitimate, but it raised the bar. It demanded a clearer why and sharper personalization, not a softer one. That tension is exactly the Strategic-AE judgment the scorer below encodes: a higher-friction lead doesn't get an easier message — it gets a better one, or none at all.

Case 02 Zillow · Systems architecture

Turning a workflow into a function.

An ICP scorer isn't valuable because it scales — it's valuable because the spine is reusable. Weighted factors in, fit score out. I proved the shape held when I left the vertical entirely, then found the one piece of data no integration can reach.

The reusable spine

Zillow ICP scoring takes weighted property-management characteristics, evaluates each against my ICP definition, and returns a fit tier. When I moved to a different problem in Cursor, I rebuilt a related sort — funding, headcount, account research → an initial outbound use case. Different inputs, different output label; same spine. That's what makes it a function, not a one-off.

The gap

The score reads external property counts fine. But existing-customer status and network-participation % live in Zillow's internal Salesforce. No Clay integration — and no Claygent lookup — can reach it, because the data isn't on the web at all. It sits behind Zillow's own auth.

The fix — an HTTP API pull

Clay initiates a request-response call to a Salesforce endpoint: send the account identifier, get back total properties and network-participation %, authed with a token in the header. Clay calls; Salesforce answers.

What it changes in scoring

> 50% participation suppress from net-new outbound — this is a customer, leave them alone.

≤ 50% participation reclassify as an expansion target — low participation is itself the opportunity.

The internal pull is the only thing that separates a "leave alone" account from an upsell. To a net-new score, the two look identical.
